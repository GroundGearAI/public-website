The honest certificate story

Your browser may say
“Not secure.” The link is still local.

GroundGear One is not connected to the public internet, so your browser cannot ask a public certificate authority to verify its local certificate. That warning is expected. The browser connection is encrypted and travels directly through the USB-C link to the device.

Practical answers

What stays where?

Does GroundGear One need the internet?+

No. The device interface, Redact processing, encrypted Vault access, and Notes assistant are designed to work locally. You may separately choose to send a redacted copy to an external AI service.

Why does the browser show a certificate warning?+

The device uses a local certificate that public certificate authorities cannot verify while it is offline. Avoiding a certificate installation step preserves portability. The HTTPS session is confined to the direct USB-C network link.

Can it restore any redacted document?+

No. GroundGear Redact can unredact files that it previously redacted when the corresponding private mapping remains on the device. It does not reconstruct arbitrary third-party redactions.

What does Vault protect?+

GroundGear Vault provides password-unlocked access to encrypted content stored on the device, within the same local browser workspace.

What can Notes do today?+

GroundGear Notes can help organize notes and search their content locally. Voice recording is planned and is not presented as a current capability.
